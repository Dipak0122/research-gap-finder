# Soil Compaction Risk in Italy: Very High Risk Class Analysis

Assessment of the *very high* (VH) risk class—defined here as covering more than 20% of a region's area—showed a clear winter–autumn persistence in eight regions: Emilia-Romagna, Umbria, Abruzzo, Apulia, Marche, Veneto, Molise, and Basilicata. Emilia-Romagna displayed the most pronounced persistence, with VH risk peaking in winter (36.0%) and again in autumn (40.1%), but dropping to near negligible levels in spring (2.0%) and summer (5.5%). This strongly "on–off" seasonal behaviour, alternating between critical and minimal VH conditions, carries important management implications: it indicates that well-timed, targeted actions during the low-risk seasons could help limit the recurrence—or escalation—of critical compaction risk. Spatially, VH risk concentrations also shifted with season. In winter, hotspots were mainly located in the northern Po Valley (Emilia-Romagna, Veneto) and along the central Apennines (Umbria, Abruzzo), whereas in autumn the VH signal broadened and extended southward, encompassing Apulia (23.9%), Molise (20.0%), and Marche (25.1%). This apparent southward progression is consistent with Mediterranean seasonality, where autumn rainfall onset and harvest schedules typically lag behind those of the more continental systems of northern Italy.
